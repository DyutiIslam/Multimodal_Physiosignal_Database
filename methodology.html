How to use the repository.

The Excel workbook is the master research database. This portal is the public discovery layer. The guide below explains how records are classified, reviewed, verified, and prepared for future systematic-review use.

Tier system Adding records ARL relevance Reading status Systematic review
Purpose

One repository, several research uses.

The repository supports ARL experiment development, dataset discovery, literature review, experimental design, related-work writing, gap analysis, and the future development of a systematic review. The Excel workbook remains the source of truth; selected metadata are published to this website.

Core principle: Tier indicates relevance to the ARL research ecosystem—not scientific quality.
Classification

The five-tier system

Use tiers to indicate how directly a resource helps us understand, design, analyze, position, or publish ARL-related research.

Tier 1

Core / Must Read

Directly comparable to ARL or essential for positioning the project.

  • Multimodal physiology
  • Group or social interaction
  • Synchronized acquisition
  • Public datasets or detailed protocols
Ask: Would understanding this materially influence how we design, describe, compare, or publish ARL?
Tier 2

Social & Group Interaction

Research centered on interaction among people, even when physiological coverage differs from ARL.

  • Conversation and meetings
  • Collaboration and teams
  • Leadership and social dynamics
  • Dyadic or multi-party behavior
Ask: Does this help us understand or analyze interactions among people?
Tier 3

Decision & Behavioral Neuroscience

Resources that explain the cognitive and behavioral processes underlying the ARL paradigm.

  • Uncertainty and risk
  • Trust and deception
  • Belief updating
  • Confidence and strategy switching
Ask: Does this explain the cognitive or decision process ARL is attempting to measure?
Tier 4

Modality & Measurement

Resources useful for acquiring, preprocessing, analyzing, or interpreting individual sensing modalities.

  • EEG / ECG / HRV
  • EDA / GSR / EMG
  • Eye tracking
  • Audio and video
Ask: Does this help us correctly acquire, preprocess, analyze, or interpret a modality?
Tier 5

Acquisition & Infrastructure

Engineering and software resources describing how multimodal experiments are implemented and synchronized.

  • LSL and LabRecorder
  • XDF and event markers
  • Clock / network synchronization
  • Multi-device recording
Ask: Does this help explain how the ARL acquisition infrastructure should work?
Categories

Tiers and research categories are different.

A paper can be Tier 3 because it supports decision neuroscience while its primary category is Deception. Recommended categories include Multimodal Dataset, Group Interaction, Decision Making, Deception, Trust, Collaboration, Social Neuroscience, Physiological Computing, EEG, ECG/HRV, EDA/GSR, EMG, Eye Tracking, Audio, Video, Synchronization, Acquisition Platform, Multimodal Fusion, Missing Modality Learning, Dataset Review, and Systematic Review.

Workbook workflow

How to add a new paper or dataset

  1. Check for duplicates.Search the Master sheet by DOI, title, and first author before creating a new row.
  2. Add bibliographic information.Record title, authors, year, venue, DOI, paper URL, and open-access URL when available.
  3. Classify the resource.Assign tier, primary category, secondary categories, and resource type.
  4. Record modalities individually.Capture EEG, ECG, EDA/GSR, EMG, eye tracking, audio, video, and other signals separately rather than writing only “multimodal.”
  5. Capture experimental characteristics.Participants, group size, task, sessions, paradigm, duration, and controlled versus naturalistic setting.
  6. Capture acquisition details.Device/model, channels, sampling rate, synchronization, recording software, file format, and event-marker method when reported.
  7. Capture dataset access.Public/restricted status, download URL, registration, license, raw/processed data, code, and GitHub link.
  8. Add research notes.Why relevant to ARL, strengths, limitations, research gaps, and likely citation use.
Scoring

ARL relevance score

5Extremely relevantDirectly comparable to ARL; usually shares multiple modalities plus group/social/decision characteristics.
4Highly relevantStrong overlap with one major ARL component.
3RelevantUseful for methodology, theory, or analysis.
2PeripheralUseful background with limited direct connection.
1ContextualPrimarily broad background or historical context.
Important: Relevance score is independent of Tier. Tier captures the role of a resource; the score captures the strength of its connection to ARL.

Target for systematic-review-quality records: Verified.

Future systematic review

Curated repository today; systematic-review database later.

The current repository should be described as a curated research database, not yet as a systematic-review database. A formal systematic review requires a documented protocol covering research questions, search databases, search strings and dates, inclusion/exclusion criteria, duplicate removal, title/abstract/full-text screening, PRISMA reporting, and quality assessment.

Recommended future field: Discovery Method

Systematic Search · Citation Chaining · Related Work · Dataset Search · Google Scholar · PubMed · IEEE Xplore · Scopus · Web of Science · Recommendation · Existing ARL Literature

Recommended future field: Review Eligibility

Pending · Include · Exclude · Background Only. Also record a standardized exclusion reason where applicable.

Source of truth

Research-to-web workflow

Original paper / dataset→Verify source→Master Excel database→Quality check→Public portal

The website should not become the master database. Excel remains the research-management source of truth; the portal is the public discovery interface.

Golden rule

Accuracy before quantity.

Do not classify papers based only on their titles. Classification should be grounded in experimental design, modalities, participants, research question, acquisition methodology, and relevance to ARL.

Fifty fully verified papers are more valuable than five hundred poorly characterized records.
