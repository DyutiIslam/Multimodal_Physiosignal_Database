Purpose & Research Context

Why this evidence repository exists

This repository was created to systematically map multimodal physiological datasets and experimental studies relevant to synchronized human decision-making, and to provide a transparent evidence base for comparing a proposed ARL-supported research study with the existing literature.

The research problem

Relevant evidence is fragmented across multiple research communities.

Multimodal decision-making research spans affective computing, social neuroscience, physiological computing, hyperscanning, deception research, group interaction, eye tracking, audio-video analysis, and multimodal synchronization.

Because these studies are distributed across different disciplines, it is difficult to directly answer a simple but important question: what combinations of sensing, interaction, decision structure, and synchronization have already been studied?

Why the repository was built

To test the research gap rather than assume it.

The repository does not begin with the assumption that the proposed ARL study is unique. Instead, it organizes published evidence so that potential novelty claims can be challenged by the strongest available comparators.

The current evidence base includes a master literature repository, a deep comparator audit, and targeted gap-challenge searches designed specifically to identify studies that could invalidate an ARL novelty claim.

Researcher

Dyuti Islam

Dyuti Islam is a PhD student at Kennesaw State University working in the Neuro Interaction Innovation Laboratory (NIIL) on research involving multimodal physiological computing, human decision-making, synchronized data acquisition, and human-centered neuroengineering.

The repository was developed as part of a broader PhD research effort to understand how multimodal physiological and behavioral sensing can be used to characterize human decision processes in interactive settings.

Relationship to the PhD

A research infrastructure, not just a website.

This repository supports the PhD by creating a reusable evidence base for experimental design, literature review, dataset comparison, acquisition methodology, and future publication.

It helps identify which aspects of the proposed ARL study are already established in prior work, which combinations are uncommon, and where a defensible research contribution may remain.

Relationship to the proposed ARL study

ARL is the motivating comparison case — not the owner of the external research listed here.

The proposed ARL-supported study involves five interacting participants completing repeated social-deduction decision rounds while multiple physiological and behavioral modalities are recorded and synchronized.

External datasets and publications in this repository were developed by their respective authors, laboratories, institutions, and publishers. They are included solely for scholarly comparison. Their inclusion does not imply that they were created, funded, or conducted by ARL or by the researcher maintaining this repository.

Current evidence question

What combination appears underrepresented?

Current evidence suggests that group decision-making, EEG hyperscanning, peripheral physiology, eye tracking, deception paradigms, explicit confidence, repeated decisions, audiovisual interaction, and synchronized acquisition all exist individually in prior work.

The strongest remaining gap is their integration within one multi-person synchronized experimental design. This remains a working evidence-gap hypothesis and should continue to be tested as the repository expands.

Long-term role

Foundation for a transparent evidence resource.

As the repository matures, it may support a formal scoping or systematic review, a dataset publication, and a living research resource for scholars interested in multimodal physiological decision-making.

The goal is not simply to accumulate papers, but to make the evidence landscape searchable, comparable, and reproducible.
